> The 39th Pacific Asia Conference on Language, Information and Computation (PACLIC 39) was held at the Vietnam Institute for Advanced Study in Mathematics (VIASM), Hanoi, Vietnam from **December 4** to **December 6**, 2025. This conference mostly focuses on bridging the gap between theoretical linguistics and computational applications. My attendance were made possible by the acceptance of my research paper.


Hanoi weather was crisp and cold, but only do you freeze your fingers when you're riding on a motorcycle. 

# I was not alone

I was lucky to have an alumnus joining me. There was also a senior colleague who graduated from Bach Khoa University; our trajectories converged because he decided to pursue a Master's degree in Computational Linguistics at my university after graduating in Electrical Engineering. I am still trying to connect the dots between these two seemingly unrelated life choices of his.

# Impression of the Conference

![welcome_kit](/paclic39/1000000239.jpg)

Arriving at the venue early to collect the welcome kit, there was literally no one there yet. The mug is my favorite item in the kit, and I have been using it often. The morning session was about the opening ceremony and keynote speeches. There were coffee breaks at 9 AM every day. I had a lot of dessert and coffee,the food was exquisite, the hot drink burned my tongue.

Lunch was covered by the conference (technically not free because it was already included in the registration fee). Must say I'm not a fan of Northern Vietnamese cuisine, it's too bland for my taste.

Across the event, most of the presentations were about linguistic analysis and language resources. I was profoundly disappointed that there were so few computational linguistics papers; most of the CS papers were about the authors' own native languages. Could not find a single paper that was innovative in terms of modern NLP methodology; I guess you can't expect much from a C-ranked conference.

To cure the boredom, I made friends with two Filipino guys. One of them was a Software Engineering graduate, and the other one used to do nursing. Talk about a career change.

# Personal Reflection

![moss_wall](/paclic39/PXL_20251206_143424193~3.jpg)

Overall, the lack of rigorous computational linguistics research throughout the conference was a letdown. The food was edible, but that was about it. In the end, the only things I genuinely enjoyed were the cold Hanoi weather, the coffee break treats, and everything else that happened outside the actual event.
